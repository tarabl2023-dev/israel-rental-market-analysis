    How the estimates workData source, price levels and photo credits

    Comfortable is the official average monthly rent from the Israel Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) for that area and size, for . It covers new and renewed contracts.

    CBS publishes averages only, not the spread of prices. Budget and luxury are rules of thumb applied to the CBS average:

    • Budget: about 80% of the average (range 70-90%)
    • Comfortable: the average (range 90-115%)
    • Luxury: about 140% of the average (range 125-170%)

    Rents vary a lot within a city, so check real listings in the neighbourhoods you like. Size bands follow CBS: 1-2, 2.5-3, 3.5-4 and 4.5+ rooms. The 4.5+ band runs from 4.5 rooms to large penthouses, so treat it as a rough guide. CBS hides 1-2 room figures for Kfar Saba and Beit Shemesh (too few contracts). Those are estimated from the city's 2.5-3 room rent and its district's price ratio.

    Ra'anana has fewer than 100,000 residents, so CBS doesn't publish its rents. Its figures use neighbouring Kfar Saba, the closest comparable city. Real Ra'anana rents may differ.

    Public transport. Station counts are train stations, light-rail stops and Haifa's Carmelit underground stations inside each city's boundary, from OpenStreetMap. Stations named after a city but just over its border (such as Kfar Saba–Nordau) count for that city. There's no official Israeli data on how rents change near stations. Studies of sale prices find homes near Tel Aviv's light rail rose faster (Globes), so the transport choice applies a rule of thumb: about +5% rent within walking distance of a station, and about −5% further out if you drive.

    What it will really cost. Everyday costs come from the CBS Household Expenditure Survey 2021 (Table 10), which reports what households spend in each sub-district. They're scaled to your household size with the CBS "standard persons" scale and brought to 2026 prices with the CBS price index for each group of goods. Cities use their own sub-district (Herzliya uses Tel Aviv's, Ra'anana and Kfar Saba use Sharon's), and other towns use their district. Some survey cells are based on few households, and missing ones use the national average. Without a car, transport is the Rav-Kav nationwide monthly pass (₪315 per adult; a ₪139 bus-only pass exists for the periphery, see Rav-Kav Online). The survey's education average includes households without children, so with children it's doubled as a rough guide. Budget and luxury lifestyles scale eating out, leisure and clothing by 0.75 and 1.5. These are estimates of typical spending, not price quotes.

    Rent figures exclude arnona (municipal tax), building committee fees (va'ad bayit) and utilities, which the cost breakdown adds separately.

    My assumptions (not from official data), so you know what is an estimate:
    • Budget and luxury rent: about 80% and 140% of the CBS average (CBS only publishes averages).
    • Transport choice: about +5% rent near a station, −5% further out if you drive.
    • Lifestyle: eating out, leisure and clothing ×0.75 (budget) or ×1.5 (luxury).
    • Education with children: twice the survey average, which includes households without children.
    • Ra'anana uses Kfar Saba's rents, and smaller towns use their district's, because CBS doesn't publish them.
